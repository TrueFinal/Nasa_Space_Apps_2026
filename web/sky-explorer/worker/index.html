Observation & quality

How to interpret this prototype

The default grayscale maps lower intensity to white and higher intensity to black. Time changes in the animation; the selected detector/channel stays fixed. Shared contrast limits help comparisons. Hatched pixels have no accepted data. Source positions are resampled onto a fixed celestial grid before comparison.

Mean stack combines the chosen time interval with variance weights. Motion trail keeps its maximum intensity at each position. Difference subtracts the reference frame: brightening is dark, dimming is light, and zero change is mid-gray. These are display products, without PSF matching; noise, artifacts and changing spectral coverage can also produce apparent changes.

A detector combines a broad range of wavelengths. A spectral channel masks pixels outside its range. Sparse data can leave holes. No missing pixels or unobserved wavelengths are invented. Calendar half-years are date bins and do not guarantee complete sky-survey coverage.

The spectrum is an exploratory 2-pixel aperture with a 4–6 pixel background annulus. Wavelengths are estimated at the field centre per exposure, and observations span different times. It is not the mission's calibrated spectrophotometry. Gaia is a positional catalogue; WISE and DSS are context images, not SPHEREx frames. Epsilon Indi B's close pair may be unresolved; a brown dwarf's absence in an individual frame does not establish a detection limit or physical classification.

Examples contain a labelled subset of real observations. Current searches enumerate the archive separately from the selected downloads. The collection list includes current DR1/QR3 wide and deep data. Partial requests and failed downloads are reported; cancellation preserves the previously loaded sequence.
